# Writing content audit · 10 October 2026

The bank contains 32 original practice tasks, not reproduced Cambridge tests or recalled exam questions. All data and places in Task 1 are fictional. Task 2 covers recurring subject areas with contemporary examples; a contemporary subject is not evidence of a future exam question.

## Primary sources

- https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources — four marking criteria, 60 minutes, 150/250 minimum words, 20/40 minute allocation, Task 2 worth two thirds, selection of main features/overview and supported relevant arguments.
- https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf — public descriptors; self-review is not an official score.
- https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/sample-test-questions/academic-test — official sample formats.
- https://takeielts.britishcouncil.org/prepare/ielts-free-practice-mock-tests/academic/writing — practice and examiner commentary.
- https://ielts.idp.com/prepare/article-common-topics-for-ielts-writing-task-2 — recurring topics, explicitly warns that questions cannot be predicted. Direct fetch returned 403 during final audit; search-index content was available.
- https://shop.cambridge.org/english/product/2700255600 — IELTS 21 Academic, four full tests and Writing samples, publication 16 July 2026. Full copyrighted bank remains with publisher.

No verified official September/October seasonal Writing question release was found. The app labels original exercises and links to authentic resources rather than representing recollections or forecast banks as official material.

## Guardrails

No rule that AI always inflates scores by a fixed amount; no automatic band cap based on templates or repetition counts. Judge against descriptors. Word count is a training aid. Drafts and annotations stay in browser storage; export is explicit. Tutor export requests evidence-based feedback, not an official IELTS result.
